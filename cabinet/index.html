What you asked for

Your current side table is a metal frame with an MDF top, and the space around the PC is overrun with a spare keyboard, several hard drives, chargers, a spare server laptop, power cords and extension boards. Nothing in the market fit: the best cabinet you found was only 13″ deep, and your PC is 14″, with no shelves inside.

So the brief is a custom piece your father can hand to a carpenter: a cabinet up to desk height (your desk top measures about 30″, desk depth 24″), the PC standing on top of it with some margin so it can't walk off in a tremor, somewhere to rest the devices plugged into the back of the PC instead of letting them dangle, shelves on the far side for small stuff, a shelf above, everything visible and reachable standing up, around 6 ft tall, with adjustable steel feet for levelling.

Why this layout

Closed below, open above. Junk you rarely touch (the spare laptop, cords, the extension boards, a small UPS) goes behind doors where dust can't reach it. Everything you touch often, and the PC itself, stays in the open rack where you can see it and grab it standing up.

Steel for the upper half, not MDF. I recommend a welded 1″ square tube frame on top of the MDF cabinet, a hybrid of your metal-frame idea and a cabinet. Tall MDF side panels would hide the PC, trap heat around it and need a second full sheet on their own. The thin steel posts leave the PC in view, let air move freely, and the steel rails run under every shelf edge, so the 32″ MDF shelves can't sag over the years (unsupported MDF is the weakest sheet material for long spans). Welders who make iron racks and gates can build this frame cheaply in any market.

24″ deep, matched to your desk. This clears your PC with room to spare in either direction. With a 14″ deep PC you get 1¾″ of margin at the front, a 3″ cable gap behind the PC, and a 5″ deep ledge at the back for whatever is plugged into the rear ports.

The cabinet stands to the right of your desk, and the PC bay is on the left. Your desk is already in place and the cabinet goes at your right hand, so the drawings are set up that way: the desk is on the left in the 3D view. The PC stands square to the front, glass panel facing straight out of the cabinet, so the fish tank reads as a display piece from anywhere in the room rather than only from one seat. The 20″ PC bay sits at the cabinet's left end, nearest the desk, which keeps the power and monitor leads short and drops them through the grommets right above the extension boards. The 9″ cubby column moved to the right end, away from the case, so nothing crowds the glass or the side intakes.

Mirror this if your desk moves. Everything here is left–right mirrored from the first version of this plan. The cut list does not change at all, because every part is the same size as before: only the positions change. If the cabinet ever has to go on the left of the desk, mirror the whole thing back and the same parts still build it.
Check one number before cutting. You wrote the PC as 14″ long, 18″ wide, 18″ high. Most tower cases are deeper front-to-back than they are wide. Put a tape on the PC side panel, front to back. If that reads 14″, this design fits as drawn. If it reads 18″, everything still fits, but cut the rear ledge (part K) to 3″ deep instead of 5″.

MDF cut list

All parts from 18 mm (¾″) MDF, ideally pre-laminated on both faces, with PVC edge tape on every visible edge. Sizes are width × depth (or width × height for panels that stand up).

How it comes out of the sheets

One 8 × 4 ft sheet is not enough. The cabinet alone needs a little more than one full sheet, so buy two sheets. The second sheet leaves a 24 × 40″ offcut for a keyboard tray or spare shelf later. Rip each sheet into two 23¾″ strips first, then cross-cut, and use the factory edges for the long 32″ parts.

Steel frame

1″ × 1″ square tube (18 gauge), welded, powder-coated or painted matt black. About 15 m of tube, 2.5 m of 1″ angle for the small shelf ledges, and 3 m of 1″ × 3 mm flat bar for the cross braces.

Earthquake and safety details

The Islamabad–Rawalpindi area sits in a high seismic zone, so the design leans on four habits. The heavy things live low: the cabinet full of cords, drives and the spare laptop is the ballast that keeps the whole unit bottom-heavy. The PC sits behind a 1″ steel lip on an anti-slip mat, with 1¾″ of margin to the front edge. The top rail is fixed to the wall with two anti-tip brackets, which is the single most effective thing you can do for tall furniture. And the top shelf at 72″ holds only light things, never anything glass or heavy.

About a UPS in the cabinet. A small desktop PC UPS on the bottom left is fine, as long as the back panel has vent holes behind it. Large inverter batteries (tubular types) must not go in a closed cabinet: they need open air for heat and gas.

Power and cables

Screw the extension boards to the inside of the cabinet's left section, directly under the PC, run their cables out through a hole in the back panel to the wall socket, and bring the PC and monitor power leads down through the grommets in the cabinet top. The rear ledge takes the USB hub, external SSD or any dongle plugged into the back of the PC. Leave the 3″ gap between the ledge and the PC for cable bends.

Build order

  1. Measure the desk top height once more, and the PC's true front-to-back depth.
  2. Carpenter builds the cabinet box: sides, bottom, divider, top, back panel, glued and screwed, with the foot blocks and T-nuts underneath.
  3. Drill shelf pin holes, cut the two grommet holes and the back vents, fit the doors.
  4. Welder builds the frame to the drawing, using the finished cabinet top as the reference so the posts land on the sides and divider.
  5. Paint the frame, screw it down to the cabinet, fit the MDF shelves on the rails.
  6. Set the feet so the cabinet top is flush with the desk, fix the top rail to the wall, then load it heaviest-first from the bottom.
