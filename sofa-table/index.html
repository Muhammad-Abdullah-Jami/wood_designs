That's about 240 sq in too much, so no layout can fit it. The table and doors fit together (left). The gun box needs a second sheet, which also leaves plenty for the dressing table later.

Cutting order for sheet 1

  1. Cutter trims 1″ off one long side and one end. Sheet is now 47″ wide × 95″ tall (3′-11″ × 7′-11″).
  2. Cross-cut at 24″: table top, 47 × 24″.
  3. Cross-cut the next 24″, then trim it to 45 9/16″ wide: table bottom.
  4. Cross-cut the next 35½″. Rip that piece at 40″: the 40″ part is the door panel, the narrow part becomes one end panel (6 11/16 × 24″).
  5. Cut the door panel through the middle: two doors, 17 11/16 × 40″ each. The ⅛″ saw cut becomes the gap between them, which doors need anyway to swing.
  6. From the bottom strip: the second end panel (6 11/16 × 24″) and the divider (22 7/8 × 6″).

Notes

  1. Overall height 16½″ is assumed. Measure your sofa's cushion height, subtract 1–2″, then subtract the 7 7/16″ box. That is your leg length.
  2. Tape every visible edge. Tape adds under 1 mm, so it doesn't change any size on this sheet.
  3. Table is now 47″ long so the top and bottom stack across the upright sheet. If the cutter trims a hair more than 1″, the top just comes out a hair shorter.
  4. Top onto ends and divider: 8 mm dowels and glue, so the top stays clean. Bottom into ends: confirmat screws through the end panels, with cover caps, plus glue. Pre-drill everything.
  5. The divider is 1⅛″ shorter than the table depth so it fits the sheet; set it back from the front, like in the photo.
  6. Mount legs on angled plates, set 3″ in from the ends and 2½″ in from the long edges, for the splay shown.
  7. Ironing only on a heat-resistant pad, never direct on the laminate.
  8. Placement clash: with the 6′-wide bed the sofa-to-bed aisle is 2′-6″. The 24″-deep table leaves only 6″ to walk past (view 2).
